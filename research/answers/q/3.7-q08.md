## Q8. What is Zstd's compression ratio vs speed trade-off compared to Snappy for Parquet files of typical Spark workload sizes? Are there benchmark references?

Zstandard's own manual describes it as "a fast lossless compression algorithm, targeting real-time compression scenarios at zlib-level and better compression ratios," supporting regular levels 1 through 22 (levels ≥20, labeled `--ultra`, requiring more memory), plus negative levels that "extend the range of speed vs. ratio preferences": "the lower the level, the faster the speed (at the cost of compression)"[^zstd-manual]. Spark exposes this level knob directly via `spark.io.compression.zstd.level` (default `1`), documented as: "increasing the compression level will result in better compression at the expense of more CPU and memory"[^spark-configuration-3]. Spark also exposes `spark.io.compression.zstd.workers` (default `0`, since 4.0.0) to spawn parallel compression threads, and `spark.io.compression.zstd.bufferSize` (default 32k, since 2.3.0), where "lowering this size will lower the shuffle memory usage when Zstd is used, but it might increase the compression cost because of excessive JNI call overhead"[^spark-configuration-3].

Spark's own defaults already treat Zstd and Snappy differently by format: ORC's default codec is `zstd` (since 2.3.0)[^spark-configuration-3], while Parquet's default remains `snappy`[^spark-configuration-3], and at least one optimization checklist in the corpus recommends overriding to `zstd`[^luminousmen-optimization-checklist]. The retrieved corpus does not contain a quantified ratio-vs-speed benchmark comparing Zstd against Snappy specifically on Parquet files at typical Spark workload sizes (e.g., X% smaller output at Y% more CPU time).
