## Q10. Does AQE interact with Dynamic Partition Pruning (DPP)? What is the order of application — DPP first, then AQE re-optimization?

Based on the corpus, DPP and AQE are separate mechanisms that operate at different points, and (at least as of the original Spark 3.0 feature write-ups retrieved here) DPP was explicitly *not* integrated into the AQE runtime-reoptimization loop. A 2020 write-up of Spark 3.0's DPP feature notes: "Even though it's not implemented yet with the Adaptive Query Execution covered some weeks ago, it's still a good opportunity to make the queries more adapted to the real data workloads"[^waitingforcode-dpp]. At that point in Spark 3.0's development, DPP ran as its own static/logical-planning-time rule (`PartitionPruning`), independent of AQE's stage-by-stage reoptimization[^waitingforcode-dpp].

Mechanically, DPP is applied during logical optimization/physical planning, before execution starts: it inserts a `DynamicPruningSubquery`/`DynamicPruningExpression` node into the plan when an equi-join is on a partition column and the pruning is judged beneficial (using distinct-count statistics or a fallback filter ratio), reusing the join's `BroadcastExchange` where possible to avoid a redundant subquery scan[^waitingforcode-dpp]. Because DPP's benefit calculation runs at planning time off static/table statistics, and AQE's re-optimization runs later, mid-execution, off completed-stage runtime statistics, the natural ordering implied by the corpus is that DPP's pruning predicate is planned first (at physical planning time), with AQE's stage-by-stage adaptive rules (coalescing, skew handling, join-strategy switching) applying afterward, during execution, on top of whatever plan DPP has already pruned[^waitingforcode-dpp][^databricks-aqe]. The exact current-version integration between DPP and AQE's reoptimization loop is not detailed further in the retrieved corpus, so treat the ordering claim as directionally supported rather than exhaustively confirmed.
