## Q9. What exactly is "external shuffle service" (spark.shuffle.service.enabled) and when is it required vs optional?

The external shuffle service (`spark.shuffle.service.enabled`, default `false`) preserves the shuffle files written by executors so that executors can be safely removed, or so shuffle fetches can continue even after an executor failure; enabling it requires setting the service up separately, since the flag alone isn't enough[^spark-configuration]. Concretely, it's a long-running process on each cluster node, independent of any particular Spark application or its executors; when enabled, Spark executors fetch shuffle files from this service instead of from each other, so shuffle state written by an executor keeps being served after that executor's own lifetime ends[^spark-job-scheduling].

It becomes effectively required, rather than optional, for dynamic allocation: a Spark executor may be decommissioned mid-shuffle (e.g. because of stragglers), and without the external shuffle service its shuffle files would otherwise have to be recomputed from scratch once the executor is removed[^spark-job-scheduling]. Dynamic allocation's own documentation lists the external shuffle service as one of several ways to satisfy this requirement: shuffle tracking (`spark.dynamicAllocation.shuffleTracking.enabled`), shuffle-block decommissioning, or a custom `ShuffleDataIO` plugin backed by reliable storage[^spark-configuration]. Outside of dynamic allocation it's optional: without it, shuffle data on deallocated executors simply remains on local disk until the application ends[^spark-configuration]. It's also currently a hard prerequisite for push-based shuffle, which is only supported for Spark on YARN with the external shuffle service enabled[^spark-configuration].
