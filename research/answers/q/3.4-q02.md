## When should repartition be preferred over coalesce, and vice versa?

Reach for `coalesce` first whenever you're only reducing partition count: it merges partitions already sitting on the same node without a shuffle, so it's the cheaper move whenever a shuffle isn't otherwise required[^definitive-guide]. Reach for `repartition` when you actually need the shuffle: increasing partition count, fixing a lopsided partition distribution, or preparing a DataFrame ahead of a join or a `cache()` call, where even parallelism is worth more than the shuffle cost[^definitive-guide].

luminousmen frames the split by what each call is *for* in practice. `repartition()` earns its cost after a heavy filter: Spark doesn't shrink the partition count when rows are filtered out, so 2,000 partitions holding 5% of the original rows just become 2,000 mostly-empty partitions, and `repartition(100)` afterward buys back real parallelism[^luminousmen-partitions]. `coalesce()` belongs at the tail of a pipeline, right before a write, purely to cut down output file count. Push it too far, though (`coalesce(1)`), and it kills parallelism outright, because coalesce doesn't rebalance data, it just stacks existing partitions together, so partitions that were uneven going in are still uneven coming out[^luminousmen-partitions]. There's a middle option, `coalesce(n, shuffle=True)`, which behaves more like repartition: you pay for a shuffle, but get real rebalancing on the way down[^luminousmen-partitions].

High Performance Spark reduces the choice to a readability rule: use `repartition` when you want a shuffle, `coalesce` when you don't, rather than leaning on coalesce's shuffle toggle to blur the line[^high-perf-spark]. At the SQL layer, both directions also have dedicated hints for controlling output partitioning without touching the DataFrame API directly: `/*+ REPARTITION(n) */`, `/*+ REPARTITION(cols) */`, `/*+ REPARTITION_BY_RANGE(cols) */`, and `/*+ COALESCE(n) */`[^spark-sql-perf-tuning].
