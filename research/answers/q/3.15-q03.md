## Q3. What is the interaction between spark.memory.fraction and spark.memory.storageFraction — are they independent, or does one constrains the other?

They are not independent: `spark.memory.storageFraction` is a fraction *of* M, and M is the region sized by `spark.memory.fraction`[^spark-tuning-guide]. So `storageFraction` works inside the pool that `memory.fraction` carves out of the heap, not as a separately-configured amount.

Within that shared region, Spark's UnifiedMemoryManager lets execution and storage borrow from each other: execution has priority and can evict storage down to the threshold R (governed by `storageFraction`), while storage cannot evict execution[^spark-notes-task-memory-management][^spark-tuning-guide]. With the defaults (`memory.fraction=0.6`, `storageFraction=0.5`), execution memory works out to usable-heap × 0.6 × (1 − 0.5) = 30% of usable heap, and storage memory to usable-heap × 0.6 × 0.5 = 30%[^luminousmen-memory-management]. Raising `memory.fraction` scales both execution and storage at once; `storageFraction` only re-splits the already-scaled pool between the two, it doesn't add memory from outside M[^luminousmen-memory-management].
